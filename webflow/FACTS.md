## Suit fittings and alterations, general guidance (sourced 2026-10-03)
- General guidance: making a suit smaller is usually doable, making it bigger is not: "taking away or reducing the
  amount of fabric is doable, but you can't make something bigger, at least not by much" (Gentleman's Gazette, "Suit
  Alterations: What a Tailor Can (& Can't) Do", Sven Raphael Schneider, posted March 27, 2024, section "How Many Sizes
  Larger or Smaller Can You Go?": https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read
  2026-10-03).
- General guidance: sleeves are among the easiest fixes: "Making sleeves slightly longer or shorter, say by a 1/2″ or
  so, is also a fairly routine procedure." Big changes look odd, because longer lengthening "puts the buttons too far
  away from the edge of the sleeve" (same article, sleeves section:
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: taking a jacket in is simple: "taking in aspects of the body of a jacket are fairly simple, and
  letting out what is available at the seams is as well" (same article:
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: trouser hems: "pant legs can be made shorter, but they can only be lengthened to the extent of
  whatever hem material is left at the bottom", and turning spare hem into cuffs, or removing cuffs, is "another easy
  alteration" (same article, section "Hems, Cuffs, and Leg Length on Pants":
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: trouser waist: there is usually "at least 2-3″" of spare fabric at the center back seam to let the
  waist out, and it can be taken in about as much; more "will affect the balance of the trousers" (same article,
  section "Adding to and Subtracting from the Waist":
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: shoulders are the part not to change: "making shoulders either bigger or smaller is not
  recommended as alterations. The structure of a jacket shoulder is complex enough that reshaping it involves major
  surgery." (same article, section "Changing the Width of Shoulders":
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03)
- General guidance: on narrowing shoulders, the same article's FAQ says "This is a very complex alteration and isn't
  always advisable since it can disrupt the jacket's balance and proportions. It's often costly and not all tailors
  will undertake this type of work." (https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/, FAQ;
  read 2026-10-03)
- General guidance: shortening a jacket is a maybe, and only by a small amount; take off too much and "the pockets and
  bottom button will be dangerously close to the bottom" (same article, section "Shortening the Jacket":
  https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/suit-alterations-what-a-tailor-can-do/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: bring the dress shoes and "a well fitting dress shirt" you plan to wear with the suit, so the
  tailor gets "the exact sleeve length" and "the right length for your trousers" (Gentleman's Gazette, "Top 7
  Tailoring Tips for Menswear", Sven Raphael Schneider, posted September 27, 2019, section "Bring Your Own Shoes and
  Shirt": https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/top-tailoring-tips-for-menswear/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance: a pinned garment fits tighter than the finished one, so test it while pinned; "you should try to
  see if you're able to move and maneuver properly" (same article, section "Once Your Garment is Pinned, Test the
  Fit": https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/top-tailoring-tips-for-menswear/; read 2026-10-03).
- General guidance for weddings: "It takes months to find a suit, get fitted, and wait for alterations. It's often
  easiest to do suit fittings as a group at the same place so that there's no confusion with timelines. Start this
  shopping process at least two to four months before the wedding to make sure that everyone gets their outfit back
  in time." (Zola, "How to Choose Groom and Groomsmen Suits", Laura Hensley, last updated February 5, 2024, section
  "If You Decide to Match": https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/how-to-choose-groom-and-groomsmen-suits; read
  2026-10-03)
- General guidance for weddings: "if you're a groom or a groomsman I would advise having it in hand a month before
  the wedding. If you do need alterations made to it, that allows ample time for a tailor to fix the sleeves or hem
  the pants" (The Knot, groom and groomsmen attire article, updated Mar 29, 2024, section "When should groom and
  groomsmen get their suits?": https://www.theknot.com/content/fashion-rules-for-grooms; read 2026-10-03)
